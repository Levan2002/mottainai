How to reduce food waste at home

Most food waste at home isn't dramatic. It's the spinach that went limp, the leftovers at the back of the fridge, the bread nobody got round to. A few small habits stop most of it, and save money too. Here are eight that work.

1. Look before you shop

Check the fridge, freezer and cupboards before you go to the shop, and write a list. Buying something you already have is one of the commonest ways to end up with food that goes off.

2. Plan meals around what's going off

Start each week's plan with the food that needs using first, not with what you fancy. A soup, stir-fry or pasta bake can use up nearly anything that's on its way out.

3. Buy what you'll actually eat

A multipack is no bargain if half of it goes in the bin. Smaller packs, loose items and more frequent shops work better for fresh food. Bulk buying is best for things that keep, such as rice, pasta and frozen food.

4. Store food properly

Keep the fridge at 4°C (40°F) or below, keep raw meat on the bottom shelf, and don't crowd it so cold air can circulate. Store potatoes and onions somewhere cool, dark and dry, and keep bananas away from other fruit because they ripen it faster.

5. Use the freezer early

The freezer is the best waste-reduction tool you own, but only if you use it before food is past its best. Bread, sliced bananas, grated cheese, cooked rice and soups all freeze well. Portion and label them, and freeze on the day you notice you won't get to them.

6. First in, first out

Put new shopping at the back and older food at the front. Keep a "use me first" shelf or box in the fridge for things that need eating soon.

7. Understand the dates

A lot of food is thrown away because of confusion over dates. "Use by" is a safety date, and "best before" is a quality date. Learn which is which and you'll bin less perfectly good food. See the guide to best before vs use by.

8. Notice what you throw away

For two weeks, pay attention to what ends up in the bin. You'll usually find the same few culprits: salad leaves, fresh herbs, bread, leftovers. Once you know them, you can buy less of them, freeze them sooner or change how you cook.

Make it a routine

None of this works if it relies on willpower. Pick one time a week, such as the evening before your main shop, to look through the fridge, move older food to the front and decide what's for dinner. Ten minutes of tidying saves far more than it costs.

Common mistakes

  • Shopping hungry or without a list. Both lead to impulse buys that don't fit any meal.
  • Hiding the old food. If you can't see it, you won't eat it.
  • Throwing out food on a best before date. Check it first; it may be perfectly good.
  • Cooking for a crowd. Cook what you'll eat, and freeze the rest straight away.
